# Prompt — stratum detection

Writing a new post means hitting the current house style. Refreshing an old one means
**migrating it out of a dead one.** This step names which one.

---

You are given: one existing page, and `strata.md` (the eras in this archive and their fingerprints).

1. **Date the page.** Publish date, last-updated date, and — more reliable than either — the
   conventions on the page itself.
2. **Match it to a stratum** from `strata.md`. Quote the fingerprints you actually found.
3. **List what must change**, separating:
   - *Mechanical* — things the linter will catch anyway (spelling variant, heading form, anchors,
     exclamation density, deprecated terms).
   - *Structural* — things it won't: no reader address at all, no subheadings, an abstract opener,
     a CTA pattern you have retired, quote attribution in the wrong place.
4. **State what to keep.** The argument, the original reporting, the examples, the quotes. A refresh
   that discards these is a rewrite, and usually a worse page.
5. **Flag remediation-not-refresh.** If the page is machine-translated, off-brand, or from a
   sub-corpus you have decided to retire, say so and stop. Some pages should be fixed or deleted,
   not lovingly rewritten in the house voice.

Output a short block at the top of the draft brief. Six lines is plenty.
